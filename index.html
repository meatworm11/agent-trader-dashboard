Research dossier: ARM — Arm Holdings PLC

Researched: 2026-06-05 · Verdict: AVOID · Conviction: 0.85 · Opportunity score: 8

Business

Arm licenses semiconductor IP — CPU instruction-set architectures and physical microprocessor designs — to chip designers who pay (a) upfront license fees for access to the technology and (b) royalties on every chip shipped. ARM v9 commands materially higher royalty rates than v8, which means ASP tailwinds persist even at flat unit volumes. Revenue is split roughly 53/47 royalties/licenses in FY2026. End markets: smartphones (~45% of chips shipped), data center (fastest-growing, royalties doubled YoY in Q4 FY2026), PC (new via Qualcomm Snapdragon X, Microsoft Copilot+ PCs, and now the Nvidia RTX Spark announcement), automotive, IoT. The durability argument is that ARM architecture is deeply embedded in the silicon supply chain — switching cost is a decade of software ecosystem lock-in, not a quarter's re-training.

Fresh thesis — is this a good buy now?

No. ARM is a great business at an untenable price. The stock has run 277% YTD to ~$393, including a ~15% single-session surge on June 1 following Nvidia's Computex announcement of RTX Spark — Arm-based Windows PC AI chips designed to run local AI agents. The fresh catalyst is real: it expands ARM's TAM in PCs, where they had limited royalty exposure historically. But a real catalyst and a good entry are not the same thing. At $393, ARM trades at 85× trailing revenue and roughly 650× trailing non-GAAP EPS. The street consensus price target sits 38% BELOW today's price (~$244 implied mean target). Even assuming 20% revenue CAGR through FY2031 and aggressive margin expansion to 35-40% non-GAAP margins, the 5-year forward math does not support $393 without requiring a premium terminal multiple that demands further multiple expansion on already historically stretched valuations. The Computex catalyst is incremental, not transformative — ARM Windows momentum (Qualcomm) already existed; this is one more tailwind, not a regime change.
